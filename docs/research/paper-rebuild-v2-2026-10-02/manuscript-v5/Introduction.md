# 1. Introduction

Medical LLM evaluation is moving beyond examination accuracy toward multidimensional assessment of real clinical tasks, safety, robustness, calibration and deployment behavior [@bedi2026medhelm; @wang2025trustworthy]. This shift is necessary but incomplete. A model can be well calibrated yet probabilistically incoherent, semantically stable yet unresponsive to clinically meaningful evidence, accurate yet unsafe under abstention policy, or action-correct for the wrong probability reasons [@xia2026calibration; @matta2026rethinking; @wu2026bas].

Most reliability research nevertheless remains organized by property. Calibration methods optimize probability quality; robustness methods target invariance; selective prediction controls answer-versus-defer behavior; logical-consistency methods regularize preference relations; medical alignment methods increasingly separate safety from general proficiency [@liu2024logic; @geng2026promedical]. This modular organization is convenient, but it leaves an operational question unresolved: **what happens to the other reliability properties when one property is deliberately improved?**

The question is especially important in medicine because multiple reliability requirements are noncompensable. Better calibration cannot compensate for an invalid deterministic risk-score action. Improved robustness cannot justify failure to respond when a red-flag feature changes. Lower selective risk is not useful if coverage collapses. An intervention that improves a scalar benchmark while degrading a safety-critical property may therefore be unacceptable even when its average score rises.

We study reliability interventions as causal objects rather than treating reliability metrics as a leaderboard. Given a base system and intervention `I_i`, we estimate its paired effect across a vector of assurance properties. Non-target cells reveal beneficial transfer, neutrality, or interference. For selected intervention pairs we estimate factorial interactions to test whether individually favorable fixes compose additively.

Our medical evaluation prioritizes tasks with exact or high-quality external oracles. Deterministic clinical calculators and executable rule logic are particularly valuable because they separate language understanding from rule execution and permit explicit threshold-crossing counterfactuals [@jin2025agentmd; @kara2025riskscores; @roeschl2026pipeline]. Separate public medical QA and evidence tasks probe properties that deterministic calculators do not define.

The intended contributions are: (1) a formal non-compensatory assurance framework with explicit non-implication witnesses; (2) a controlled reliability intervention matrix; (3) a preregistered interaction design for selected reliability fixes; (4) exact medical-rule oracle families and transformation contracts; and (5) independent-family replication with complete negative-result reporting. Each contribution remains conditional until its falsification gate is satisfied.
