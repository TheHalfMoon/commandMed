# 7. Discussion

The central question is not whether a system can score highly on many reliability metrics, but whether engineering one reliability property changes the others in predictable and acceptable ways. If targeted fixes produce material non-target harms, reliability engineering should move from isolated optimization toward constrained, profile-aware development. If effects are consistently negligible under adequately powered equivalence tests, that result is also valuable because it supports modular intervention design for the studied families.

A non-additive interaction would have a distinct implication. It would show that individually favorable fixes cannot be safely composed by assuming their benefits add. Conversely, replicated near-zero `Gamma` under meaningful margins would justify a narrower modularity claim.

The non-compensatory assurance analysis is intentionally conservative. In high-stakes settings, a strong average can hide a catastrophic weak coordinate. The conjunction-based rule does not identify a uniquely correct deployment threshold; it makes the policy choice explicit and prevents compensation from occurring silently.

Deterministic clinical tools are treated as reference mechanisms, not replacements for medical judgment. Their role is to provide exact arithmetic or rule execution where such a rule is already accepted, thereby allowing the study to isolate extraction, calibration, routing and decision behavior. This boundary is supported by prior calculator and pipeline studies [@jin2025agentmd; @roeschl2026pipeline].

The study should not be interpreted as proving clinical safety. It evaluates declared reliability properties on declared task distributions. Clinical deployment would require external validation, workflow integration, human factors, population transportability, privacy, regulatory and prospective outcome evidence beyond this paper.
