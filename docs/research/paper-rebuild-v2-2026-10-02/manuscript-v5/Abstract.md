# Abstract

Medical foundation-model reliability is often improved and evaluated one property at a time: calibration, semantic robustness, logical coherence, abstention, evidence sensitivity, action validity, or retained capability. Yet these properties are not interchangeable, and recent work shows that strong performance on one reliability notion can coexist with failure on another. What remains insufficiently tested is whether **interventions** designed to fix one reliability property transfer safely to the others.

We propose a controlled study of **cross-property reliability interference**. For each targeted intervention, we estimate a paired intervention matrix over independently reported assurance properties, using source-case clustering, prespecified meaningful-effect margins, confirmatory quarantine, and non-compensatory safety gates. We additionally test selected intervention pairs for non-additive interactions and compare conventional compensatory scoring with a conjunction-based assurance rule.

The medical substrate emphasizes deterministic clinical-rule oracles, executable decision logic, and explicitly licensed public tasks so that action validity can be distinguished from language plausibility. Interventions include post-hoc calibration, selection-bias correction, typed decision readouts, semantic-contract regularization, selective act/defer policies, and deterministic clinical-rule routing, subject to exact reproducibility and matched-comparison constraints.

The study is intentionally falsifiable. A major claim requires a replicated material non-target effect, a replicated non-additive interaction, a strong equivalence result under prespecified margins, a consequential aggregate-versus-hard-gate reversal, or a newly derived Pareto-improving intervention with independent confirmation. Empirical results are omitted until the protocol is frozen and execution is separately authorized.
